# GIS-Based Land Use, Forest Change, and Multi-Criteria Residential Development Suitability Analysis in the Rhein-Sieg District and Meckenheim, Germany
  
**Study Area:** Rhein-Sieg District and Meckenheim, North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany  
**Focus Areas:** GIS · Spatial Analysis · Land-Use Planning · Multi-Criteria Analysis (MCA)

## Overview

This project applies Geographic Information Systems (GIS) to investigate land-use patterns, historical forest-area change, residential building land change, and potential locations for future residential development in the Rhein-Sieg District and Meckenheim, Germany.

The project combines spatial mapping, change analysis, and **multi-criteria analysis (MCA)** to identify potential residential development locations based on environmental constraints and accessibility to essential services and public transport.

## Project Highlights

- **Land-use mapping:** Visualisation of land-use and land-cover patterns across the Rhein-Sieg District.
- **Forest change analysis:** Assessment of forest-area change between 1845 and 2018.
- **Residential change analysis:** Examination of residential building land change in Meckenheim between 2018 and 2022.
- **Multi-criteria suitability analysis:** Identification of two potential residential development locations in Meckenheim.

## Approach and Methods

The project combined three main stages of GIS analysis:

- **Spatial mapping and change analysis:** Mapped land use across the Rhein-Sieg District, assessed forest-area change between 1845 and 2018, and examined residential building land change in Meckenheim between 2018 and 2022.
- **Multi-criteria suitability analysis:** Applied environmental and land-use constraints, proximity requirements, and accessibility criteria to identify two potential residential development locations in Meckenheim.
- **Cartographic presentation:** Produced thematic maps to communicate land-use patterns, historical changes, and the results of the suitability assessment.

### Suitability Criteria

The residential development suitability assessment considered the following criteria:

| Criterion | Applied requirement |
|---|---|
| Environmental constraints | Exclude protected areas and forests |
| Existing land use | Exclude existing settlements and traffic areas |
| Industrial separation | 500 m buffer around industrial areas |
| Railway accessibility | Within 40 minutes by e-bike |
| Kindergarten accessibility | Within 15 minutes on foot |
| Supermarket accessibility | Within 15 minutes by car |
| Proximity to residential areas | Within 100 m of existing residential zones |

The criteria were used to screen for areas meeting the specified requirements. Two potential residential development locations were identified.

- Spatial datasets representing land use and environmental constraints

**GIS techniques**
- Land-use and land-cover mapping
- Historical spatial change analysis
- Buffer and proximity analysis
- Network-based accessibility assessment
- Multi-criteria suitability modelling

- Land-use map of the Rhein-Sieg District.

## Limitations

The results depend on the quality, completeness, and temporal consistency of the input datasets. Accessibility estimates depend on the transport network data and travel-time assumptions, while suitability results are influenced by the selected criteria and thresholds.

The two identified locations should be interpreted as **potentially suitable under the criteria applied**, not as officially approved development sites. Further assessment of planning regulations, land ownership, environmental conditions, infrastructure capacity, and site-specific constraints would be necessary before any development decision.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how GIS can be used to map land-use patterns, investigate historical forest change, assess residential building land change, and identify potential locations for future residential development.

By integrating environmental constraints and accessibility requirements, the multi-criteria suitability analysis illustrates how spatial methods can support land-use planning and preliminary site assessment.
